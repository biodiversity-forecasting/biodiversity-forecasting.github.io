The Centre for Biodiversity Forecasting (CBF) will give public authorities and private companies a science-based toolkit to assess, predict and report how planned decisions on land use and natural resources shape future biodiversity. CBF is one of four national [Centres for Sustainable Land Use and Nature Management](https://www.forskningsradet.no/forskningspolitikk-strategi/ltp/senter-barekraftig-areal-naturbruk/), initiated by the Ministry of Climate and Environment and funded through the Research Council of Norway together with several ministries, from 2026.

The centre is a collaboration between NTNU, UiT The Arctic University of Norway, other national and international research partners, and a wide range of private and public user partners. It is led from the NTNU Gjærevoll Centre.

## Why we need forecasts for nature

The climate and nature crises reinforce each other, and the demand for land and sea area is growing. Under the Kunming–Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework, Norway has committed to, among other things, conserving 30 per cent of land and sea by 2030.

Yet we know little about how the concrete choices we make affect the nature we want to protect:

- **Climate policy has a common unit**, CO₂ equivalents. Nature management lacks an equivalent "currency".
- **Species data are biased** in time, space and across species groups, and today's knowledge system does not correct these biases.
- **We cannot separate the effects** of climate change and natural development from those of local land and resource use.
- **Reporting tools** assume that more area means more biodiversity, and that this relationship is stable over time. This hides local differences and non-linear change.

In a time of rapid climate change, "untouched nature" is no longer a useful reference either. We need to look ahead and separate what we can act on from what we cannot control.

## Goals

<div class="cards" markdown="1">
<div markdown="1">
### Scientific goal
Develop projections that clearly separate the effects of climate change, human activity and natural ecosystem processes on biodiversity.
</div>
<div markdown="1">
### Societal goal
Turn the forecasts into practical tools for decisions and reporting, together with public and private actors, so that the time from planning to decision can be shortened while nature is better safeguarded.
</div>
</div>

The tools build on three things:

- **Scenario-based projections** showing the consequences of current and planned land and resource use, separated from climate change and natural development.
- **Decision-relevant indicators** expressing the combined effect of multiple pressures in a set of comparable "currencies" for biodiversity.
- **Visible uncertainty** showing which knowledge is solid, what is missing, and where new fieldwork pays off most.

## What it means

The aim is not to remove conflicts over land use. They may reflect real trade-offs. The aim is to move the debate from disputes over what we know to discussions about what we want to prioritise.

Centre management is based at the NTNU Gjærevoll Centre, and the management group also includes representatives of the partner institutions SINTEF Energy Research, the University of Oslo, Nord University and Statistics Norway.

| Body | Role |

## A solid foundation

- Strong basic research groups, including two Norwegian Centres of Excellence (SFF) and several European Research Council (ERC) grants.
- Unique observation systems in the Trondheimsfjord area, on the Helgeland coast and in the Mjøsa region, and the Climate-ecological Observatory for Arctic Tundra (COAT).
- A broad partner network across academia, business and public authorities.

The centre also works with the sister projects TRACE, InterPlay, AID and COAT.
